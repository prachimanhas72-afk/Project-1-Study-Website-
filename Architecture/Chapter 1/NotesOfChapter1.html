Structural Design And Systems

Notes

1) Introduction

  • Structural design is the process of planning and designing the structural elements of a building so that it can safely carry all expected loads and remain stable, durable, and functional throughout its life.

  • Building systems refer to the coordinated technical systems that make a building safe, comfortable, functional, and efficient. These include structural, mechanical, electrical, plumbing, fire-safety, environmental, and communication systems.

  • Architecture combines form, function, structure, materials, services, and environmental considerations into one integrated design.

2) Importance

Structural design is important because it:

  • Provides strength and stability to the building.
  • Transfers building loads safely to the ground.
  • Prevents excessive deformation, cracking, and collapse.
  • Determines the possible span, height, form, and proportions of a building.
  • Influences the selection of construction materials.
  • Helps achieve economical and efficient construction.
  • Contributes to the architectural character of a building.
  • Ensures resistance to environmental forces such as wind and earthquakes.

A successful architectural design should not treat structure as an element added after the design. Architecture and structure should be developed together.

3) Types Of Structural Systems

The major types are:

  1. Load-Bearing Wall System
    • Walls carry the building loads.
    • Common in low-rise buildings.
    • Materials: brick, stone, concrete blocks.

  2. Framed Structure
    • Loads are carried by beams and columns.
    • Walls mainly act as partitions or enclosure.
    • Common in residential, commercial, and institutional buildings.

  3. Rigid Frame System
    • Beam-column joints resist bending and lateral forces.
    • Suitable for multi-storey buildings.
