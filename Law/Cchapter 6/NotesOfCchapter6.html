Constitutional Law

Notes

1) Introduction

  • Constitutional Law is the branch of law that deals with the Constitution, structure and powers of government, and rights and duties of citizens. It explains the relationship between the legislature, executive, and judiciary and establishes limits on governmental power.

  • It also deals with important principles such as fundamental rights, separation of powers, rule of law, judicial review, federalism, and constitutional supremacy.

2) Importance

Constitutional Law is important because it:

  • Protects Fundamental Rights: It protects the basic rights and freedoms of citizens.
  • Limits Government Power: It places limits on the powers of the government and prevents misuse of authority.
  • Establishes Government Structure: It defines the powers and functions of the Legislature, Executive, and Judiciary.
  • Promotes Democracy: It provides the framework for democratic government, elections, equality, and rule of law.
  • Ensures Justice and Equality: It promotes justice, liberty, equality, and dignity and provides remedies when constitutional rights are violated.

3) Types Of Constitutional Law

The major types are:

  1. Unwritten Constitution
    • Constitutional rules are based on laws, conventions, customs, and judicial decisions.
    • Example: United Kingdom.

  2. Federal Constitution
    • Powers are divided between the central and state governments.
    • Both levels have constitutionally defined powers.

  3. Unitary Constitution
    • Major powers are concentrated in the central government.
    • Local or regional governments receive their powers from the centre.
