Law Of Contract

Notes

1) Introduction

  • The Law of Contract deals with agreements that are legally enforceable. A contract is an agreement between two or more parties that creates legal obligations.

  • For a valid contract, there must generally be offer and acceptance, lawful consideration, free consent, competent parties, lawful object, certainty, and an intention to create legal relations. It determines the rights and duties of the parties and provides legal remedies when contractual obligations are not fulfilled.

2) Importance

Law Of Contract is important because it:

  • Protects Rights: It protects the legal rights and interests of parties entering into agreements.
  • Creates Certainty: It provides clear rules regarding the rights and obligations of contracting parties.
  • Facilitates Business: It helps in conducting business and commercial transactions smoothly.
  • Provides Remedies: It provides legal remedies, such as damages and specific performance, when a contract is breached.
  • Promotes Trust: It creates confidence and trust between parties by making legally valid promises enforceable by law.

3) Types Of Law Of Contract

The major types are:

  1. Valid Contract
    • A contract that satisfies all the essential requirements of law.
    • It is legally enforceable by the courts.

  2. Voidable Contract
    • A contract that is enforceable at the option of one party.
    • It usually arises due to coercion, fraud, misrepresentation, or undue influence.

  3. Implied Contract
    • The contract is created through the conduct or circumstances of the parties.
    • The terms are not expressly stated but are understood from the situation.
